How it works

Where every piece of data comes from, and what happens at each step.
1

Read the company's website

The homepage is fetched with a plain web request (robots.txt is checked first; a page that comes back almost empty is retried through a text-reader service). Navigation, footers and cookie banners are stripped and the text is capped.

Source: the company's own public websitecached 7 days
2

Pull out the facts

Gemini turns the page text into named fields: what they sell and to whom, funding stage, headcount, named customers, certifications, enterprise features, open roles. Certification badges are also found with a pattern scan of the raw HTML.

Source: step 1 textmodel: Gemini
3

Qualify, with an agent loop

Code checks which facts are still missing (certifications, is it B2B SaaS, stage) and fetches the one page most likely to answer each - careers, pricing, security or about - up to the limit in the rubric. Then the rubric decides: qualified, flagged, dormant (already certified), disqualified, or manual check. Every point has a written reason.

Source: up to 4 more pages of the same siterules: Filters & rubric
4

Find the person to write to

Contacts are not scraped from the web. They come from one of three places: the Hunter.io domain-search API (switched off for now), a contact you add by hand after looking them up in Apollo (the POC_OVERRIDES secret - the brief's Option B), or a contact passed in with a test case. Without one, the company stops at "No contact".

Source: Apollo lookup by you, Hunter.io, or the test input
5

Two pain points

Gemini names the two problems this person most likely feels about compliance, each tied to a specific fact from step 2.

Source: steps 2 and 4
6

Write the email

Emails are written, not fetched. Gemini drafts a UK-style email addressed by first name, opening with a researched fact, using the best-ranked feedback and winning emails for that role. Quality checks test its structure; the disagreement score checks for wrong or unsupported claims. Above the threshold it goes to Review.

Source: steps 2-5 + feedback repoReview
7

Send - to your own inbox only

Sent through Gmail (SMTP with an app password). The To: address is always your own inbox, as the brief requires; the intended recipient is written in the footer.

Gmail SMTPTo: your inbox
8

Replies, meetings, follow-ups

Replies are read from Gmail (IMAP). Interested: free times are taken from Calendly and offered, or the time they named is booked. Declined: a thank-you, and they become a cold lead. No reply: up to three follow-ups with new angles, then non-respondent. Each finished conversation is turned into lessons for the feedback repo.

Gmail IMAPCalendlychecked every 30 min

Where it runs

This dashboard and the test-case API run on Vercel; the batch run and the reply checker run on GitHub Actions. Everything is stored in a Neon Postgres database.

What you do

Give the list of domains, add contacts from Apollo, review drafts above the threshold, add feedback, and tune the rubric.

What it never does

Email a prospect directly, log in to any website, or guess a fact it did not find - unsupported claims raise the disagreement score.
